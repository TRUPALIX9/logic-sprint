A · Revive comeback

Three seconds to get ready

Problem found

  • _watchAd() passes onReward: _engine.revive, so the run is live the moment the ad closes. In Rocket Launch the storm is already moving before you've found the ship.
  • Heart revives do the same.
  • The 5 s offer ring is fine, but there's no breathing room on the other side of it.

Requirement

  • After any revive: a 3-second "get ready" countdown. A dark scrim fades out slowly while 3 · 2 · 1 pops in the middle, then play resumes.
  • The run clock stays stopped during it (it doesn't count toward time-based games).
  • Pause or leaving the app during the countdown restarts it from 3. Reduced motion: digits only, no scale.
  • A light haptic tick on each number, a stronger one on "go".

How

  • RoundEngine.revive() moves to a new RunState.resuming instead of playing; stopwatch stays stopped. New engine.go() starts it.
  • RoundScreen shows a GetReady overlay on resuming: one 3 s AnimationController drives scrim opacity 0.85 → 0 (ease-out) and swaps the digit every second; on complete calls go().
  • Games need no change: onRevive() already clears danger; onResume() work moves to go().
  • Tests with fake_async: elapsed time unchanged across the countdown; pause at 1.5 s restarts at 3.

B · Privacy at a glance

Bring the website's data map into the app

The site's /privacy page opens with "Where your data lives": every piece of data as a chip that sorts itself into Your phone, Leaderboard, Ads or Never collected as you scroll. The app's Privacy screen only renders the markdown. Put the same map in the app with a switch between the two views.

Requirement

  • Privacy opens on At a glance; a segmented switch swaps to Full policy (today's text, unchanged).
  • Layout: a one-line summary, then one card per place (Your phone, Leaderboard, Ads, Never collected), each with an item count, a one-line note and its items. Items land one by one when the page opens; Never collected items are struck through.
  • Same buckets and wording as the website so the two never disagree. Settings → Delete my data linked from the Leaderboard bucket.

How

  • New assets/brand/docs/privacy_map.json: buckets and chips with icons. Both the website and the app read it, so it's written once.
  • PrivacyScreen gets a SegmentedControl and a DataMap widget; chips use AnimatedSlide + AnimatedOpacity with a 60 ms stagger (same as rank rows).
  • Golden test for both tabs.

D · Sprint Score

A 0–100 rating that one game can't game

You asked for "you're a top 2% player" with a 0–100 score, hidden below 75, and fair across games. The trick is to never add raw scores across games (a Rocket Launch score is ten times a Guess Color score) and to make one spike count for less than steady skill.

The maths, in four steps

1. Per board (game × difficulty), your percentile among everyone on it: p = 100 × (players below you + ½ × players tied with you) ÷ players on board Mid-rank for ties, so a shared top score is never "100". Ordered exactly like the boards (score, then time for Memory Lane and Quick Math). 2. Per game: your best board for that game. Easy boards are capped at 90, Medium at 97, so Hard is the only way to the very top. A game counts once you have ≥ 3 runs in it. 3. Combine with shrinkage toward the middle (k = 2 imaginary games at 50): R = (p₁ + p₂ + … + pₙ + 2 × 50) ÷ (n + 2) One game at 99 → R = 66. Four games at 90 → R = 83. Spikes count, consistency counts more. 4. Sprint Score = your R's percentile among all rated players (0–100). "Top X%" = 100 − rating, rounded UP to 1, 2, 5, 10, 15, 20 or 25 so it never overclaims. Shown only when rating ≥ 75, you have ≥ 2 rated games and ≥ 50 players are rated.

Problems to handle

  • 46 players today (site stats). "Top 2%" of 46 is one person, so the ≥ 50 rated-players gate keeps it hidden until it means something.
  • Boards with few players give jumpy percentiles: a board counts only with ≥ 30 players.
  • Impossible scores are already zeroed by record_run, so they can't push anyone's percentile.

Requirement

  • Rating and per-game percentiles on the Trophy case and the share card.
  • Below 75 or not enough data: show progress ("Rate 1 more game to get a rating") instead of a number.
  • Updates with the boards, once a day at 00:00 UTC.

How

  • Materialized view player_ratings using count() windows per board, refreshed by pg_cron at 00:00 UTC.
  • RPC my_rating() → rating, top %, per-game p, rated players. Fetched with the daily boards fetch.
  • Same formula in Dart for a unit test that cross-checks the SQL on fixtures.

The stats card sits at the top of the Profile tab, so you see it every time: your name and code, Sprint Score and your best in every game. Under it comes the full breakdown: total runs, time played and revives, then each game's best with its board rank, top %, runs and best time. At the bottom sits one Share stats button. What goes on it

With every share goes one line of text: Can you beat my LogicSprint score? logicsprint.trueorfalse.dev. The same link is printed on the card, so it survives apps that drop the text.

How

  • The Profile card and the shared image are the same Flutter widget. For sharing it's drawn off-screen in a RepaintBoundary at the target size and saved as a PNG with toImage(pixelRatio:). No ads are ever in it.
  • One Share stats button on Profile opens a bottom sheet with the four choices and a live preview. Story and Snap use 1080 × 1920, Post 1080 × 1080 (games in two columns), More 1080 × 1920 through the system share sheet.
  • Phase 1: all four buttons open the system share sheet (share_plus) with the right image, so it works now with no extra keys. Instagram and Snapchat appear in that sheet.
  • Phase 2: Story jumps straight into Instagram Stories (needs a Meta app ID) and Snap straight into Snapchat's camera (Snap Kit Creative Kit, needs Snap's review). Each button falls back to the sheet if that app isn't installed.
  • Made on the phone, nothing uploaded, so the privacy policy doesn't change. The same Share row also appears on the Result screen and on History rows.

G · Loading screen

Launch into the games

Problem found

  • SplashScreen shows the logo and an indeterminate bar for a fixed 900 ms timer. The bar measures nothing.
  • Real start-up work (Storage.open, PackageInfo, Supabase init, network check) runs in main() before runApp, behind the native launch screen.
  • If the native launch screen and the first Flutter frame don't line up, the logo visibly jumps.

How

  • LOGICSPRINT sits in the centre. From it, one circuit line per game runs out to that game's corner in the game's own colour, with a light pulse travelling along it (like the CircuitBackground traces, now alive).
  • Each corner shows a quiet hint of its game: a Memory Lane grid, a Quick Math sum, a Guess Color word in the wrong ink, an Angle Sense angle. It lights up when its line reaches it.
  • Above it all, the Rocket Launch ship (same painter as the game, rocket_look.dart) hovers and tows the LOGICSPRINT wordmark up on a rope made of 0s and 1s. The rope starts slack, pulls taut as the wordmark rises into place with a small overshoot, then both sway gently together while the bits flow up the rope toward the rocket.
  • Faint stars twinkle slowly behind; nothing else moves.
  • The bar becomes real: move Supabase init and the network check into the splash, and fill the bar as each step finishes. Keep a 900 ms minimum and a 2.5 s cap.
  • Match the native launch screen (iOS LaunchScreen.storyboard, Android 12 splash) to the first frame: black with the logo at the same spot.
  • The fly-by takes 2.4 s and the bar fills with real loading steps; reduce motion shows the finished frame with all four hints.

H · I · Newer asks

Heart straight to continue, clean screenshots

H · Earned a heart? Continue the run

  • Problem found: on the Result screen "Earn a heart · Watch ad" adds a heart and leaves you on Result. The run is already over, so the heart can only be used next time.
  • Requirement: if the run could still be revived (revives left, score ≥ 50, online), earning the heart goes straight back into that run: heart spent, 3 · 2 · 1 get-ready, play on.
  • How: keep the finished engine alive behind Result for a short window instead of disposing it. Result gets a continueRun callback; after the reward it pops back to RoundScreen and calls engine.reviveFromOver() (a new path from over → resuming that also removes the run from History and holds the score submit until the run really ends).
  • Watch out: a run must not be submitted twice to the leaderboard, so submit moves to when Result is left.

I · Screenshots without ads, with your name

  • Platform limit: neither iOS nor Android lets an app change the screen before a system screenshot is taken. iOS only tells the app afterwards (userDidTakeScreenshotNotification), Android 14 the same (ScreenCaptureCallback). So the system screenshot will always include the banner.
  • What we can do: when the app sees a screenshot, show a small "Share a clean version" chip. It renders the same screen again, in-app, with the banner replaced by your name and code (NEON_FOX#0420), and opens the share sheet.
  • Also: the Share button (stream E) and Result screen always produce ad-free images, so most sharing never needs the system screenshot.
  • How: a RepaintBoundary around the page body; the banner slot swaps to a PlayerTag widget while capturing; Android needs no extra permission, iOS none either.
J · Top 10 that scales

‹ Game › on top, difficulty underneath

Today the Ranks tab picks a game from one row of equal segments ("Rocket · Memory · Math · Color"). With Angle Sense, Line Split and True or False that row needs 7 slots and the names stop fitting. The new picker shows one game at a time, big, with arrows (and swipe) to move through them. Dots show how many games there are, so 7 or 17 work the same. Easy / Medium / Hard tabs sit right under it, only for games that have them.

Problem found

  • ranks_tab.dart builds the picker from GameId.values as equal-width segments with short titles ("Rocket", "Memory", "Math", "Color"). At 375 pt width each segment is about 80 pt, and 7 games would make each one about 46 pt.
  • The difficulty row appears and disappears under it, so the board jumps up and down between games.

Requirement

  • ‹ Game › switcher: game icon, full name and accent colour; arrows on both sides, swipe on the name or the board, and it wraps around.
  • Dots under it, one per game, current one wide and in the game's colour.
  • Easy · Medium · Hard tabs always take the same space. Games without levels show one "One board" tab in the same spot, so nothing jumps.
  • Each switch animates: old rows slide out the way you swiped, new rows slide in staggered (30 ms apart), and scores count up from 0 to their value.
  • The last game and difficulty you viewed are remembered.

How

  • A PageView (or GestureDetector + index) over GameId.values for the name strip; the board below listens to the index. Adding a game needs no layout change.
  • Rows reuse the existing RankRow and its rank-change animation; switching game runs the same staggered entrance with a direction.
  • Count-up via TweenAnimationBuilder<int> (600 ms, ease-out). Reduce motion: rows appear at once, no count-up.
  • Accessibility: arrows are 44 pt buttons labelled "Previous game" / "Next game"; the name strip announces "Angle Sense, 5 of 7".
